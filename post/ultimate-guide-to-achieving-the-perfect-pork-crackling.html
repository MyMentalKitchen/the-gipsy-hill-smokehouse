How To Get Perfect Pork Crackling

Why it goes leathery, and what actually fixes it.

You can hear good crackling from across a garden.

That is the test, and it is the only one that matters. Not the colour, not the photograph. The sound it makes when it goes. Good crackling shatters. Bad crackling bends, and everyone is too polite to say so.

Most of what goes wrong happens hours before the pig gets anywhere near a fire.

What crackling actually is

Pork skin is water, fat and collagen. To turn it into crackling you have to get the water out and the fat rendered, and then hit what is left with enough heat that it puffs and sets hard.

Do those in the wrong order and you get leather. Skin that still holds water will steam itself no matter how hot your fire is — you are effectively boiling it under its own moisture. That is the single most common reason crackling fails, at home and on the professional rigs.

So the whole job is: dry it, render it, then blast it.

2. Scoring

Scoring gives the fat a way out and gives the skin somewhere to move as it tightens.

Tim — your method How do you score, how deep, and how far apart? Do you score a whole hog at all, or only the joints? And what happens if someone cuts through into the meat — is that the mistake it is made out to be?

3. Salt

Salt pulls water out of the skin, which is why it is a drying tool as much as a seasoning one.

Tim — your method What salt, how much, when does it go on, and does any come off again before cooking? Oil or no oil? People online are very confident about this and mostly contradict each other, so I would rather have yours.

4. The fire

This is where a whole hog stops resembling anything that happens in a domestic oven. A hog is cooked for hours at a temperature that would be far too low to crackle anything, and the skin has to survive all of it in a condition where it can still be brought up at the end.

Tim — your method Roughly how long is a hog on for, and at what sort of heat? Where is the fire in relation to the skin, and how does that change through the cook? Is the crackling made at the end in a final blast, or is it being managed the whole way through? This is the part nobody writing about crackling online has actually done, so the more specific you are the better.
